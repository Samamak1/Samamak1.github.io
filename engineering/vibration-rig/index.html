Vibration sensing and data acquisition

I built and wired a two sensor vibration rig and recorded three channels in LabVIEW. The final data set contains 1,022 samples. Repeat trials, channel swaps, and connection checks helped isolate wiring and mounting faults.

Where
Wichita State University, ME 633
When
Spring 2016
Role
Fixture, wiring, acquisition, and analysis
Tools
LabVIEW, Arduino Uno, two sensors, and Excel

Test setup and method

The Keyestudio KS0037 supplied analog and digital outputs. The MiniSense 100 piezo element supplied a second sensor response. I assembled the mounts and test fixture, wired the sensors to an Arduino Uno, and recorded all three channels in LabVIEW. The final file contains samples 4310 through 5331.

Each trial followed the same process. I excited the surface, captured the channels, plotted the response, and checked whether the same event appeared across the sensors. When one channel disagreed, I swapped the input, reseated the connection, checked the mount, and repeated the trial.

The recorded peaks were 3.57 volts on the Keyestudio analog output, 4.99 volts on the MiniSense, and 4.17 volts on the Keyestudio digital output. In this run, the Keyestudio analog response showed a longer decay after each event. The MiniSense returned to baseline faster, which made separate events easier to distinguish.

The side by side traces made troubleshooting possible. A response that appeared on only one channel could be checked against the wiring, input assignment, and sensor mount before it was treated as a surface event.
