Kite buggy design and structural analysis

A five person ME 439 project that modeled a portable 6061 aluminum buggy against a 30 pound weight limit, a $400 parts budget, and a ten foot drop case. I shared the load, stress, and deflection calculations with two teammates.

The requirements called for a buggy that fit in the trunk of a small car and was designed for speeds up to 40 mph. The controlling load case used a 30 pound cart, a 180 pound rider, a 0.25 second impact, and six inches of seat travel. The impulse model estimated a 3,694 pound load on the frame.

We compared aluminum, steel, and titanium for section size, weight, and cost. Aluminum 6061 T451 met the limits with a two inch outside diameter and a 0.25 inch wall. The rear axle was 28 inches wide and the cart was 36 inches long.

Under the modeled drop load, the rear axle carried about 1,850 pounds of shear and 9,330 inch pounds of bending moment. Calculated deflection was 0.061 inches and calculated stress was 17,378 psi against a 21,000 psi yield strength. That produced a design factor of 1.21. Under normal riding loads, the rear axle design factor was 20.3.

The tipping model included kite pull angle, pull height, rider movement, and an assumed dry asphalt friction coefficient of 0.7. Under that assumption, the model predicted sliding before tipping.
