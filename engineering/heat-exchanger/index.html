Shell and tube heat exchanger design study

An individual design study that sized a countercurrent water to oil shell and tube exchanger from requirements to dimensions and compared ten materials across a clean surface and two fouling conditions.

Where
Wichita State University, ME 750C
When
May 2016
My role
Individual project, method, calculations, and report
Scope
Heat duty, flow regime, coefficients, material selection, area and length, cost

The question

A heat exchanger is only as good as the heat it moves for the space it takes and the money it costs. The paper asked how much effectiveness could be gained on one common type, a shell and tube exchanger with countercurrent flow, by working the design correlations carefully and choosing the material and the dimensions on evidence instead of habit.

The method

  • Set the requirements, inlet and outlet temperatures, mass flow rates, and allowable pressure drop.
  • Pull fluid properties at the bulk temperatures.
  • Calculate the required heat transfer rate.
  • Use the Reynolds number to settle whether each stream is laminar or turbulent, developing or fully developed.
  • Use Nusselt number correlations for the inside and outside coefficients, with the log mean temperature difference and the countercurrent correction factor.
  • Build the overall coefficient for a clean surface, then repeat with fouling resistances for seawater cooling below and above 325 K.
  • Run ten candidate materials through the same equations and eliminate on conductivity, cost, and the resulting area and tube length.

Baffles were kept in the design for the three things they do at once. They slow fouling from impurities, they support the tube bundle, and they raise the outside coefficient by forcing cross flow. Their cost showed up in the pressure drop, which is why the pressure drop sat in the requirements from the first step.

The result was a set of dimensions for each condition, clean, fouled cold, and fouled hot, so the trade between a compact exchanger and a tolerant one was explicit rather than hidden in a single answer.
