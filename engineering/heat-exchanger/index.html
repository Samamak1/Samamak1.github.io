Shell and tube heat exchanger design

For an individual ME 750C project, I sized a countercurrent water to oil shell and tube heat exchanger and compared ten materials under clean and two fouling conditions.

Where
Wichita State University, ME 750C
When
May 2016
Role
Method, calculations, comparison, and report
Scope
Heat duty, flow, coefficients, area, and tube length

Method

I began with the inlet and outlet temperatures, mass flow rates, and allowable pressure drop. I used fluid properties at the bulk temperatures to calculate heat duty, identified each flow regime with Reynolds number, selected the appropriate Nusselt correlations, and calculated the inside and outside heat transfer coefficients.

I combined those values into an overall coefficient and repeated the analysis with fouling resistance for seawater cooling below and above 325 K. Each of the ten materials went through the same calculation so conductivity, cost, required area, and tube length could be compared on the same basis.

Baffles remained in the design to support the tube bundle and direct shell side crossflow. Pressure drop stayed in the requirements because stronger crossflow also increases pumping demand.

The final deliverable was a documented analytical design with dimensions and material comparisons for all three surface conditions.

Working calculations

This was an analytical course project and was not built or physically tested.
