Mechanical design at MKEC Engineering

This was the year when mechanical engineering moved from the classroom into real buildings. I worked from architectural plans and site conditions, calculated heating and cooling loads, prepared coordinated schedules, and helped resolve the conflicts that appeared between a drawing and the field.

The work

I supported commercial, residential, and modular projects. Each one started with a different building, but the work followed a clear path. I reviewed the architectural plans, worked through heating and cooling loads, selected and scheduled the equipment, and prepared the mechanical information the wider project team needed.

My drawings and schedules covered HVAC, plumbing, electrical coordination, equipment, and airflow. I worked with architects, engineers, contractors, and field conditions so the documents could move from design into construction.

How a project moved through my desk

Read the building first

I began with the plans, spaces, use, orientation, and site conditions so the load calculation reflected the building in front of me.

Turn calculations into documents

I carried the engineering into equipment and airflow schedules and into coordinated AutoCAD and Revit drawings that other disciplines could use.

Resolve what the drawing missed

Site visits exposed clearances, routing, and coordination problems. I brought those conditions back into the design and checked the update against the applicable codes and standards.

Building a faster Revit workflow

Repeated work was taking too long because common elements were being recreated project by project. I standardized Revit families, templates, and repeatable details so the team could begin from a useful base instead of starting over. The change reduced design time by about 25 percent while keeping the documents consistent.

What the role taught me

A calculation is only one part of a building project. The work also depends on clean drawings, accurate schedules, code checks, coordination with other disciplines, and the willingness to visit the site when the answer is not visible from a desk. That is the kind of practical engineering work I want to return to.
